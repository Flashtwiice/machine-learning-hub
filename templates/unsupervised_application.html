1. Dataset and Variable Selection

Topic: smart-meter energy consumption. Utilities collect readings from thousands of households but rarely know in advance which consumer types exist, so this is a natural unsupervised problem: there is no label, only behavior to be grouped.

  • Average daily consumption (kWh): measures how much energy a household uses, the main driver of cost and grid load.
  • Peak-hour consumption (kWh): energy used in the 6–10 pm window; it captures when energy is used, which is what determines demand peaks and tariff design.

Together they separate households by both volume and timing. Both are numerical, continuous and measured in the same unit.

Why K = 3?
{% for r in k_comparison %} {% endfor %}
KInertia (SSE)Silhouette
{{ r.k }}{{ r.inertia }}{{ r.silhouette }}

Inertia drops sharply up to K = 3 and much more slowly afterwards (the "elbow"). K = 2 has a slightly higher silhouette, but it merges the evening-peak households with the high-consumption ones, hiding a behavior difference that matters to a utility. K = 3 was chosen as the best balance between separation and interpretability.
