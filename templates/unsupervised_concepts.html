UNSUPERVISED MACHINE LEARNING

Concepts: Clustering and K-Means

Finding structure in data when there are no labels to predict.

What Is Unsupervised Learning?

In unsupervised learning the dataset has no target variable. The algorithm receives only input variables and must discover hidden patterns, groups or structure on its own, instead of learning to reproduce known answers as supervised models do.

What Is Clustering?

Clustering is the most common unsupervised task: it groups records so that those inside the same cluster are similar to each other and different from the records in other clusters. Typical uses are customer segmentation, anomaly detection and usage profiling.

How Does K-Means Work?

K-Means partitions the data into K clusters, where K is chosen in advance. Each cluster is represented by its centroid: the mean position of all the records in it.

1
Initialize

Choose K starting centroids.

2
Assign

Send each record to its nearest centroid (Euclidean distance).

3
Update

Move each centroid to the mean of its records.

4
Repeat

Iterate steps 2–3 until assignments stop changing.

The Role of Centroids

A centroid is the "center of gravity" of a cluster. It is the reference point used to decide membership, and its coordinates describe the typical record of the group. Its starting position matters: poor initial centroids can lead to slower convergence or a worse local optimum, which is why scikit-learn uses k-means++ initialization and several restarts.

The Role of Iterations

Every iteration can only keep or reduce the within-cluster sum of squares, so the algorithm steadily improves and eventually converges, when centroids stop moving. Convergence is usually reached in a handful of iterations, as the manual exercise shows.

Why Standardize the Data?

K-Means relies on distances, so a variable measured on a larger scale dominates the result. Standardizing puts all variables on a comparable scale before clustering.

Evaluating Clusters: Silhouette Score

The silhouette score compares how close a record is to its own cluster versus the nearest other cluster. It ranges from −1 to 1: values near 1 mean well-separated clusters, values near 0 mean overlapping clusters.
